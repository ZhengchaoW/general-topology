Separation and countability

We see that a general topological space can behave weirdly compared to metric spaces. We want to impose some additional conditions on topological spaces to make them “nicer” and closer to metric spaces. Eventually, we would like to answer the following question: when can a topological space be embedded into a metric space? Of course not all of them as we’ve seen previously. We will see that the answer is related to separation axioms and countability axioms.

Countability

Recall the notion of neighbourhoods.

Definition 5.1 (Neighbourhood) Let (X,T)(X,\mathcal{T})(X,T) be a topological space and x∈Xx\in Xx∈X. A set B∈TB\in \mathcal{T}B∈T is a (open) neighbourhood of xxx if x∈Bx\in Bx∈B.

Definition 5.2 A space XXX has a countable basis at a point x∈Xx\in Xx∈X if there is a countable collection of neighbourhoods B={Bn:n∈N}\mathcal{B}=\{B_n:n\in\mathbb{N}\}B={Bn​:n∈N} of xxx such that for every neighbourhood VVV of xxx, there exists nnn with Bn⊆VB_n\subseteq VBn​⊆V.

Example 5.3 Let XXX be equipped with the discrete topology. Then XXX is first countable: for each x∈Xx\in Xx∈X, the collection {{x}}\{\{x\}\}{{x}} is a countable basis at xxx.

Separation Axioms

We’ve seen such an example before and I claimed that this appears because the points are not “separated” enough.

Example 5.17 Let X={a,b}X=\{a,b\}X={a,b} with the topology τ={∅,{a},X}\tau=\{\emptyset,\{a\},X\}τ={∅,{a},X}. Then the constant sequence (xn)(x_n)(xn​) with xn=ax_n=axn​=a for all n∈Nn\in\mathbb{N}n∈N converges to aaa and bbb.

Let’s see some different ways to separate points and sets in a topological space and check if they can help us to avoid such pathological examples.

Definition 5.18 We say a topological space XXX is Hausdorff if for any two distinct points x,y∈Xx, y \in Xx,y∈X, there exist disjoint open sets UUU and VVV such that x∈Ux \in Ux∈U and y∈Vy \in Vy∈V.

Proof. Let x∈Xx\in Xx∈X. To show that {x}\{x\}{x} is closed, we show that its complement X∖{x}X\setminus\{x\}X∖{x} is open. In fact, metric spaces satisfy stronger separation axioms. We will introduce some of them.

Definition 5.27 Let XXX be a topological space such that one-point sets are closed. We say that XXX is:

  1. regular if for any point xxx and a disjoint closed set BBB, there exist disjoint open sets UUU and VVV such that x∈Ux\in Ux∈U and B⊆VB\subseteq VB⊆V.

  2. normal if for any two disjoint closed sets AAA and BBB, there exist disjoint open sets UUU and VVV such that A⊆UA\subseteq UA⊆U and B⊆VB\subseteq VB⊆V.

Remark 5.28 A regular space is Hausdorff. A normal space is regular (hence Hausdorff).
